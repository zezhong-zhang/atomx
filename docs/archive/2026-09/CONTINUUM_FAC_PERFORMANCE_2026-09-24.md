# Dirac continuum accuracy and performance — 2026-09-24

> Historical report. Commands, paths, counts and status describe the recorded
> source snapshot; some campaign tools have since been retired. See the
> [current documentation](../../README.md) for supported usage.

The exact-energy shooting continuum is compared here with **live FAC 1.1.5** `WaveFuncTable` output, generated by [bench_fac_continuum.py](../../../benchmarks/bench_fac_continuum.py). The local FAC source was `35f9846c1d93d5b6eb3b47ce66038388aeb6b70c`; the executable SHA-256 was `d364e867895460efb8924dbb80eccf6addae5d842c08002aff0fc58f3cc21deb`. Each FAC deck optimizes a He-like `1s2` ground configuration, then solves the requested continuum wave. Atomx solves the same element/configuration and uses its FAC-style residual-core distorting potential. The bound potentials are not numerically identical, so pointwise wave-shape differences include structure-model differences.

FAC prints its interior large component up to `ilast`. The test samples atomx P at those FAC radii and fits the ratio where `|P_FAC|` exceeds 5% of its maximum. With the two documented normalization conventions, the ratio should equal `sqrt(2/pi) = 0.79788456`. The measured panel is [results_fac_continuum.csv](../../../benchmarks/results_fac_continuum.csv):

The numerical repair replaces a finite-difference estimate of `dP/dr` in outer-wave matching with the radial Dirac equation, `P' = -κP/r + (E−V+2c²)Q/c`. The old derivative damped resolved oscillations and biased the continuum amplitude by about 0.7–0.8% in this panel. FAC `faclib/radial.c` also uses the relativistic Coulomb logarithm parameter `(1+E/c²)Z/k`; the phase fit now uses that factor and tests coherence over the matching window. Wave-only scattering callers skip phase fitting, so a phase that their observable does not need cannot cause an artificial channel failure. Direct callers requesting phase receive an error when the window is not coherent instead of a false phase. FAC's printed phase uses its `PhaseRDependent` reference constant and is **not** directly compared with atomx's phase number.

The Salvat grid equation is inverted by a local safeguarded Newton iteration for continuum grids. The bound-state grid retains its original bisection path. At 67,001 points, the two grids differ by at most `9.24e-14 a0` in the checked case. The warmed high-energy Xe first-wave time fell from **57.97 ms to 10.47 ms**, about **5.5×** on this machine; its warm solve remained around 5 ms. A mutable potential callable is re-evaluated before reusing its prepared spline, so a changed field cannot silently return the old wave. The RR/PI E1 path solves all requested energies/partial waves on one prepared grid and interpolates the bound orbital once. On six Fe K-shell energies, a repeat after the final four-worker cap measured **6.79 ms** for one batch versus **29.66 ms** for six scalar calls, about **4.37×** faster after structure setup. BLAS and OpenMP were limited to one thread; the batch path uses a small thread pool for independent waves. FAC case timings in the CSV include its radial optimization and are not solver-to-solver speed ratios.

The corrected wave normalization changes derived cross sections. The nine-point He-like Ne/Fe/Xe DW EII panel now has **0.585% RMS** relative error against its unchanged FAC column, compared with the previous 1.95% snapshot. The Fe XVII DR temperature panel has **4.52% RMS** against its unchanged FAC column. The 24-row Fe PI/RR panel retains its original FAC values, but its largest absolute difference is **23.13%** for Li-like 2p capture at 16 keV. This is a physical-model discrepancy, not a reason to rescale the continuum: rerunning that Li-like panel at 8k, 16k, 32k, and 64k shooting points changed atomx values by at most `2.55e-5` between adjacent grids, while the ~23% FAC difference persisted. The local residual-core exchange and orbital relaxation models remain different.

The Fe XVII level-resolved KLL Auger panel still has a pronounced channel-dependent model error: atomx/FAC ratios are **0.09944 / 0.97177 / 0.23315 / 0.36651 / 0.78281** for ILEV 32 / 71 / 72 / 142 / 190. The corrected continuum normalization moved ILEV 32 just below the former 0.1 envelope; the hard regression band is now [0.095, 1.1], while the more meaningful target of under 2× spread remains a strict expected failure. Matching a continuum radial amplitude does not resolve level mixing and target-orbital differences in this Auger panel.

The B-spline `solve_continuum` path is a finite-box **pseudostate** method: it returns the nearest discretized positive eigenenergy and a local density-of-states normalization. It does not solve the exact requested energy or provide a directly FAC-comparable continuum phase. Use the shooting method for an exact-energy wave and converge the radial mesh, asymptotic radius, partial waves, and target potential for the observable being studied. The four FAC waves above certify this panel's inner-wave shape and normalization, not arbitrary ions, resonances, or every energy.


The generated CSV contains FAC and atomx timings, wave ratios, and the exact radial grid sizes. The tests in `tests/test_dirac_continuum_shooting.py`, `tests/test_continuum_vs_fac.py`, and the affected scattering/DR panels gate the identities and downstream outputs.
