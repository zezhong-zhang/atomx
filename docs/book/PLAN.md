# Book Series Plan: *Theoretical Atomic Physics — Define, Derive, Calculate*

*(Working title. Note: H. Friedrich's Springer book is already titled "Theoretical
Atomic Physics" — decide early on a distinguishing subtitle or a new main title,
e.g. "Atomic Physics, Executed" or "Computational Theoretical Atomic Physics".)*

Companion code: **atomx** — every equation lands in a runnable, tested
implementation; every number is regenerated by CI.

---

## 1. The thesis: what a textbook is *for* in the age of AI

Atomic physics already has canonical texts: Bethe–Salpeter, Sobelman, Cowan,
Grant, Johnson, Friedrich, Fischer–Brage–Jönsson, Drake's handbook. Any
derivation in them can now be regenerated on demand by an AI. A new book cannot
compete on *coverage of derivations*; it competes on what AI and existing books
are both bad at:

**(a) Ground truth, not reference.** Every quantitative claim is an executable
test. Figures, tables, and quoted numbers regenerate from atomx in CI against
NIST ASD, FAC, and published experiments. *The textbook has a test suite* — any
sentence can be falsified by a reader or their AI assistant.

**(b) Conventions as first-class content.** Condon–Shortley phases, continuum
normalization (the √(2/π) story), gauge choices, Rydberg-vs-Hartree traps —
precisely where atomic codes disagree and where LLMs hallucinate confidently.
The "convention contract" is promoted from footnote to load-bearing, tested
appendix.

**(c) The quantified approximation ladder.** Every chapter ends with an
ablation table (computed live by `atom.climb()`): what each rung costs in CPU
and buys in accuracy at low/medium/high Z. Approximation *judgment* is the
skill AI can't substitute and books never teach explicitly.

**(d) Verification epistemology.** When plausible-but-wrong numbers are free,
"how do you know this number is right?" is the central scientific skill.
Clean-room parity protocols, pinned grids, error budgets — and an honest
chapter of real war stories from atomx's own development (fabricated-benchmark
incident, MCDHF limit cycles, the continuum-normalization root cause).
Failures are pedagogy; no textbook publishes theirs.

**(e) Problem-first structure.** Volume I opens with the Fe XVII 3C/3D
intensity puzzle (in the test suite vs Bernitt 2012) and spends the volume
building the machinery to compute it, closing the loop in the final chapter.

**(f) Model × engine orthogonality.** atomx decouples physics model (HF
nonlocal, Slater-α, Gu local exchange) from numerics engine (B-spline
Galerkin, Salvat shooting). Making the matrix explicit teaches the most common
question in computational physics: "is this discrepancy physics or numerics?"

**(g) A living book.** Notebook per chapter, figures are code, versioned with
the library, errata are pull requests, DOI per release.

**Chapter template (uniform across the series):**
**Define → Derive → Calculate (atomx) → Verify (NIST/FAC/experiment) → Ablate.**

## 2. The series architecture: three cultures + the bridges

Since ~2000 atomic physics has split into three cultures that barely read each
other's books:

1. **Structure/spectroscopy** (Cowan, Grant, GRASP, FAC; astro & plasma
   consumers) — what atomx serves today.
2. **Precision** (bound-state QED, clocks, fundamental tests) — lives in
   review articles, not textbooks.
3. **Control** (cold atoms, quantum optics, Rydberg arrays, ion/atom qubits) —
   Foot/Metcalf/Pethick–Smith, which treat atomic *structure* as a lookup table.

Nearly every frontier result sits at an intersection: clocks need MCDHF-grade
structure (BBR shifts, magic wavelengths); neutral-atom quantum computers need
C₆'s, differential polarizabilities, decay budgets; Th-229 nuclear clock, HCI
clocks, King-plot new-physics searches need all three at once.

**Series thesis: one computational structure engine as the connective tissue
across all three cultures — and outward, into molecules, condensed matter,
and radiation/particle transport.** The same sum-over-states machinery that
computes a polarizability becomes a magic wavelength, a clock error budget, a
Rydberg blockade radius, a gate fidelity, a van der Waals C₆, a stopping
power. No prior book could show that pipeline because no prior book had a
live engine.

### The four volumes

- **Vol. I — The Atom: Structure, Radiation, Collisions, Plasmas.**
  The current atomx-native plan (§3 below). The verification spine.
- **Vol. II — Precision: QED, Nuclei, Clocks, and New Physics.**
- **Vol. III — Control & Dynamics: Light, Coherence, Cold Atoms, Strong Fields.**
- **Vol. IV — Bridges: From the Atom to Molecules, Matter, and Transport.**
  Many-body methods (DFT, QMC, neural), molecules, condensed matter,
  photon/electron/neutron transport.

Shared spine across volumes: the convention contract, the CI-tested-claims
production model, the ablation-table discipline, and atomx (plus its
volume-specific extensions, §7).

Vol. I chapters gain small **"bridge boxes"** — half-page pointers showing
where a Vol. I object reappears downstream (e.g. Ch. 19 oscillator strengths →
Bethe stopping power, Vol. IV; Ch. 8 Slater exchange → Kohn–Sham LDA, Vol. IV).

## 4. Volume II — Precision (chapter table, draft)

1. Bound-state QED properly: Furry picture, S-matrix, one- and two-loop
   self-energy — what Vol. I's radiative potentials approximate.
2. NRQED and effective field theory; α-expansions for He and Li.
3. Hydrogen and the Rydberg constant; spectroscopy of simple systems.
4. Muonic atoms and the proton-radius story.
5. The bound-electron g-factor: Penning traps, the electron mass, α.
6. Highly charged ions: QED at strong fields, HCI clocks.
7. Optical clocks I: principles; lattice and ion clocks; magic wavelengths.
8. Optical clocks II: the systematic error budget, *computed* — BBR shift,
   lattice light shifts, micromotion — reproducing a published clock budget.
9. Symmetry tests at experimental precision: EDM and PNC with statistics and
   systematics, not just enhancement factors.
10. New physics with atoms: variation of constants, dark-matter searches with
    clocks/comagnetometers, King-plot nonlinearity as a boson probe.
11. Antimatter atoms: positronium, muonium, antihydrogen gravity (ALPHA-g).
12. The Th-229 nuclear clock and the electron bridge (laser excitation 2024).
13. Superheavy elements and supercritical fields — is Og a noble gas?

*(Capstones: each chapter reproduces one published measurement's theory side.)*

## 6. Volume IV — Bridges: Molecules, Matter, Transport (chapter table)

**Framing: the atom is the calibration laboratory of many-body physics, and
the periodic table is the API between atomic physics and the rest of science.**
Every electronic-structure paradigm can be taught, tested, and *falsified* on
atoms — where near-exact answers exist — before being exported to molecules
and solids; and atomic cross sections are the microphysics inputs to every
photon/electron/neutron transport code.

### Part A — The Many-Body Method Space (one atom, four paradigms)
1. **The Calibration Laboratory** — near-exact few-body answers: Hylleraas
   helium, correlated Gaussians; the He atom as the "MNIST of electronic
   structure."
2. **DFT I: From Thomas–Fermi to Kohn–Sham** — Hohenberg–Kohn, KS equations;
   atomx's Slater-α and Gu exchange re-read as proto-DFT; the average-atom
   model as DFT-in-a-cell. *(atomx already contains 80% of an atomic KS solver.)*
3. **DFT II: Jacob's Ladder, Judged on Atoms** — LDA/GGA/hybrid vs HF vs CI vs
   exact, per Z, computed live; self-interaction error made visible (the H
   atom in LDA); why atomic total energies are the canonical DFT benchmark.
4. **Quantum Monte Carlo I: Variational MC** — Slater–Jastrow trial functions
   *built from atomx orbitals*; Kato cusp conditions (derived, then tested);
   Metropolis sampling, local energy, zero-variance principle.
5. **QMC II: Diffusion MC** — imaginary-time projection, fermion sign problem,
   fixed-node approximation; *measuring* the nodal error on He/Be/Ne against
   the deterministic ladder; finite-T sketch (PIMC).
6. **Learned Wavefunctions** — FermiNet/PauliNet as the neural trial-function
   frontier; atoms as the eval set; where stochastic meets learned.
7. **The Method Map** — deterministic (HF→CI→CC) × stochastic (VMC→DMC) ×
   density (LDA→hybrid) × learned (neural): one atom, four columns, one live
   cost/accuracy/scaling table. *No existing book teaches all four paradigms
   on the same system with running code.*

### Part B — From Atom to Molecule
8. **Born–Oppenheimer and Two Centers** — H₂⁺ exactly, H₂ correlated;
   potential curves, avoided crossings (the charge-exchange machinery revisited).
9. **LCAO and Basis Sets** — quantum chemistry's Gaussian basis sets as
   contracted atomic orbitals — literally products of atomic physics;
   Wigner–Witmer rules tying molecular states to atomic term symbols.
10. **Molecular Consumers of Atomic Data** — ultracold molecule formation,
    precision molecular spectroscopy (eEDM in ThO/HfF⁺ — bridge to Vol. II).

### Part C — From Atom to Condensed Matter
11. **The Radial Solver Inside Every Solid** — muffin-tin potentials,
    APW/LAPW/LMTO as atomic radial solvers embedded in crystals; generate a
    pseudopotential live from an atomx Dirac–Fock run.
12. **Cores in Crystals** — XPS/XAS/EELS edges; crystal-field multiplet theory:
    Cowan's atomic code as the workhorse of condensed-matter X-ray
    spectroscopy (Thole, de Groot lineage); Hubbard U as a screened atomic
    F⁰ — the Slater integrals of Ch. I.10 running quantum materials.
13. **Magnetic Form Factors** — ⟨j₀⟩, ⟨j₂⟩ from atomx radial orbitals; what
    neutron diffraction actually measures of the atom.

### Part D — Transport: the Atom as Microphysics Supplier
14. **The Linear Boltzmann Equation, Once** — photons, electrons, neutrons as
    one transport framework differing only in their cross-section inputs;
    Monte Carlo vs deterministic solution; where atomic physics enters each.
15. **Photon Transport** — opacities, Rosseland/Planck means, radiative
    transfer; the iron opacity problem (Sandia Z) and kilonova/lanthanide
    opacities as live research bottlenecks fed by Vol. I machinery.
16. **Electron Transport** — the generalized oscillator strength as the
    universal object: Bethe stopping power *derived from the TRK sum rule over
    atomic oscillator strengths*; inelastic mean free paths; elastic DCS from
    the distorted-wave machinery; Monte Carlo dose transport — the
    PENELOPE/Geant4 world, whose radial solver (Salvat) *is* atomx's shooting
    engine. The lineage comes full circle.
17. **Neutron Interactions** — what is nuclear vs what is atomic: scattering
    lengths, thermalization and moderation, Debye–Waller, magnetic scattering
    via Ch. 13 form factors; ultracold neutrons.
18. **S(q,ω), the Rosetta Object** — one dynamic structure factor uniting
    EELS, inelastic X-ray scattering, Compton profiles, and stopping power —
    all computable from atomic wavefunctions.

### Unifying threads stated explicitly in Vol. IV
- **Sum rules as connective tissue**: TRK links oscillator strengths (I.19),
  polarizability (I.16), stopping power (IV.16), and opacity (IV.15) — one
  identity spanning four fields, verified once in code, cited everywhere.
- **One radial engine, four disciplines**: the same solver underlies atomic
  structure (GRASP/FAC), all-electron DFT (WIEN2k/ELK cores), pseudopotential
  generation, and electron-transport MC (PENELOPE).
- **Error budgets travel**: the Vol. I ablation discipline applied to DFT
  functional choice, QMC nodal error, and transport data uncertainty —
  uncertainty quantification as the common language.

## 7. Companion-code roadmap (per volume, honestly scoped)

**For Vol. II** — clock-error-budget module (BBR, magic wavelengths — largely
buildable from existing polarizability machinery).
**For Vol. III** — one-electron TDSE solver (small, self-contained, highest
pedagogical value per line); OBE/master-equation layer or a QuTiP bridge
(don't own quantum optics); C₆/C₃ dispersion coefficients from existing
sum-over-states code; model-potential cold collisions (single channel +
Feshbach toy — do *not* own molecular potentials).
**For Vol. IV** —
- Atomic Kohn–Sham solver: add LDA/GGA correlation functionals to the existing
  local-exchange SCF — *small*.
- VMC/DMC mini-engine for atoms (numba, few hundred lines) — *small/medium*.
- Pseudopotential generator (Troullier–Martins-style) — *medium*.
- GOS → stopping-power module: mostly exists via Born-completion machinery — *small*.
- Magnetic form factors ⟨j_L⟩: quadratures over existing orbitals — *trivial*.
- Compton profile / S(q,ω) — *medium*.
- Two-center H₂⁺ solver (pedagogical) — *small*.
- 1D Boltzmann / Monte Carlo transport demo — *small*.
Out of scope to own: full quantum chemistry, periodic DFT, quantum optics
stacks — interface and cite instead.

## 8. AI integration — six layers

0. **Reproducible book** (baseline): every claim a test, figures are code.
1. **The book as agent environment.** An MCP server exposing atomx, the
   datasets, and the benchmark harness. A reader's AI answers by *computing*,
   not recalling; the book defines the tool surface and the convention
   contracts that make tools trustworthy. The book stops being text an AI
   reads and becomes a world an AI acts in.
2. **Machine-checkable derivations.** Every load-bearing derivation ships a
   symbolic companion (SymPy) verifying each step, units, and limiting cases —
   derivation tests beside numeric tests. A few crown jewels optionally in
   Lean, flagged experimental.
3. **Adaptive reading paths.** The concept dependency graph authored as
   metadata; the printed order is one traversal among many; AI tutors render
   per-reader paths (astro reader skips cold atoms; QI reader skips plasmas).
   This is what basics-to-beyond-PhD span actually requires.
4. **Exercises as verified challenges.** Auto-judged by the harness ("compute
   X within Y of the pinned reference"). Designed AI-*collaborative*: the
   graded skill is directing and verifying an AI, not hand-deriving. Capstones
   reproduce published numbers with error budgets. QMC chapters are the
   natural home for teaching statistical uncertainty — error bars are native.
5. **A living frontier.** The beyond-PhD chapters are open problems with
   baseline code, pinned data, public metrics (DR width parity; a lanthanide
   opacity table; the Th-229 electron bridge rate). Book as research onramp,
   DOI per release.
6. **Dual-audience authoring.** Definitions, conventions, claims as typed,
   machine-readable sidecar objects. Side effect: the exercise suite becomes
   an executable physics eval for AI models — a benchmark grounded in computed
   truth. The book contributes *to* AI evaluation, not just uses AI.

**What stays irreducibly human:** taste in approximation, the narrative arcs
(Bohr → Dirac → Lamb → clocks → new physics), error-budget judgment, and the
verification culture — taught through atomx's own published failures.

## 9. Production model

- Book text in-repo (`docs/book/`); figures/tables generated under
  `docs/book/figures/`, gated by `pytest -m book`; notebook per chapter,
  executed in CI; no hand-typed physics numbers in prose — all injected from
  computed artifacts at build time.
- Jupyter Book / Quarto → living HTML + citable versioned PDF releases
  matching atomx versions.

## 10. Risks / open decisions

- **Title collision** with Friedrich (decide early).
- **Scope discipline**: Vol. I ships first and alone; Vols. II–IV are designed
  now but written serially. Vol. I must not grow bridge content beyond the
  half-page bridge boxes.
- **Honesty gaps**: any chapter whose parity is weaker than its prose (e.g. DR
  radiative widths 1.09–1.27× FAC) needs code improvement or explicit
  error-budget treatment — the series lives or dies on never overclaiming.
- **Vol. IV competence risk**: DFT/QMC/transport each have their own expert
  communities; the volume's defense is that it teaches these methods *on
  atoms, against exact answers* — territory where the atomic-physics author
  has home advantage — and defers bulk applications to the native literature.
- **FAC comparisons**: verification target, not derivative work (clean-room
  protocol already documents this).

## 11. Writing order (not reading order)

1. Vol. I Ch. 21 (continuum contract) — strongest existing material.
2. Vol. I Ch. 33 (verification) — already documented.
3. Vol. I Ch. 2–4 (hydrogen + numerics) — locks the template cheaply.
4. Vol. I Ch. 0 + 32 (Fe XVII bookends) — locks the narrative arc.
5. Remainder of Vol. I outward from the spine.
6. Vol. IV Part A (DFT/QMC on atoms) is the best-decoupled later start — it

   discipline; it can be drafted in parallel with late Vol. I chapters.
