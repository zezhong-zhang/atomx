# NIST ASD verification of atomx excitation energies

Generated by `benchmarks/verify_nist_levels.py`; every number below is a measured residual against the committed NIST ASD v5.12 files (or a statistic computed from them). Nothing here is estimated.

## 1. What is compared, and what is not

NIST ASD energy levels are **relative to the ground state of the ion** and carry no absolute total energy, so the comparison is always

```
ΔE_computed(level) − ΔE_NIST(level),   ΔE = E(level) − E(computed ground state)
```

An atomx *total* energy is never compared with a NIST number. The computed ground state is the lowest state of the model space at the same rung, so every rung is internally consistent.

## 3. Rungs

| rung | how it is computed |
|---|---|
| `DHF` | one jj-average Dirac-Fock SCF **per configuration** (`Atom.solve(DiracFock(breit=False, qed=False))`); every level of a configuration shares that configuration's energy, so this rung cannot represent any fine structure or LS splitting |
| `DHF+B+QED` | the same with `DiracFock(breit=True, qed=True)`; the Breit + QED shift is evaluated per configuration, so it *does* move excitation energies |
| `DHF+MBPT2` | the `DHF` rung with the **closed-shell ground configuration** replaced by `E_DHF + E2` (`atomx.methods.mbpt2_energy`). The excited states are unchanged, so this rung is explicitly composite and is reported as such |
| `MCDHF` | one CI over the model space (`Atom(config=[...], corrections="none")`), which splits each `(2J, parity)` block |
| `MCDHF+B+QED` | the same with Breit + QED |

### 3.1 Model space

Each system declares a candidate list of full configurations. The model space actually used is the **largest sub-list whose `(2J, parity)` block dimensions balance exactly** against the NIST levels of those configurations that carry both a single J and an energy (block dims from `enumerate_jj_csfs`, summed over configurations). It is therefore limited by what NIST resolves, not hand-tuned.

### 3.2 Pairing, and how the pairing validates itself

Within a block both lists are sorted by energy and paired positionally. That is only safe if the computed states are the same physical states, in the same order, as the observed ones — so the driver additionally requires the energy-ordered sequence of each computed state's **dominant CSF configuration** (the l-resolved occupation of the leading CI vector) to reproduce the energy-ordered sequence of NIST configurations. A block that fails either the dimension or the sequence test is excluded and counted in the `excl. blocks` column; it is never silently re-paired.

`mode="block-min"` (Fe I, N I, O I) pairs the lowest `k = min(n_calc, n_nist)` states of a block because NIST's observed list is a strict subset of the configuration's LSJ space, and requires the first unpaired computed state to lie above the last paired NIST level.

`MCDHF` (the average-level CI over the same space) is **much worse than the single-configuration `DHF` rung for these one-electron ions** — for H I, rms 2,132 cm⁻¹ against 48 cm⁻¹, with the computed 2p₁/₂ and 2p₃/₂ orbital energies at −0.36162 Ha and −0.35181 Ha against the exact Dirac eigenvalues −0.125002 Ha and −0.125000 Ha. The s orbitals come out exact (−0.50000666 Ha, exact to 5e−10), so the defect is specific to the fractional-occupation Guerra/AL mean field acting on the p and d channels of a one-electron system: `Σ qᵢ = 1` with `qᵢ < 1` leaves a spurious inter-orbital Coulomb/exchange term that a one-electron system cannot have. It is reported, not worked around — see §9.

### 5.6 Breit + QED is a no-op at the CI rung (measured)

`MCDHF+B+QED` reproduces `MCDHF` level-by-level to 1.49e-08 cm⁻¹ (worst case: Fe XXV). That is not an accident of this panel: the MCDHF solver evaluates Breit + QED once on the converged average-level orbitals and adds the same scalar to every CI state (documented in `atomx/methods/mcdhf/__init__.py`), so excitation energies are invariant while absolute energies shift. The non-scalar (spin-dependent) part of the Breit interaction is therefore absent from the CI rung, and **QED improvement can only be measured at the single-configuration rungs**.

## 8. What this panel does NOT verify

* **Transition probabilities are parsed but not verified.** The panel holds 23,049 observed lines, of which 20,216 carry an `Aki`. The lines reader is exercised by the tests (counts, vacuum/air flag, `Ei`/`Ek`, configuration and term fields), but this report makes no claim about atomx `Aki` values — that is a separate piece of work.
* **Levels NIST prints without a J, or with a J group** (`0,1,2`) or as a term placeholder (`-----`), are excluded from every comparison: `(2J, parity)` is undefined for them. The per-ion counts are in §3.3.
* **Levels NIST prints without an energy** are excluded (N I: 12, O I: 30, and several ions have 1–2); they cannot be compared with anything, and their absence is what forces `block-min` pairing for N I and O I.
* **Ionisation energies, isotope shifts and hyperfine constants** are not in the levels/lines files used here and are not verified.
* **Absolute total energies** cannot be verified against NIST at all; only differences within one ion are.
* **Rydberg / autoionising series** above the first ionisation threshold are outside the model spaces used here.
## 9. Findings about atomx (evidence, not opinion)

1. **Average-level MCDHF is wrong for one-electron systems.** For H I the CI rung gives rms 2,132.41 cm⁻¹ against 47.55 cm⁻¹ for single-configuration DHF; the computed 2p₁/₂ orbital energy is −0.36162 Ha against the exact Dirac −0.125002 Ha (1s: exact to 5e−10 Ha). Minimal reproduction:

   ```python
   from atomx import Atom
   a = Atom(1, config=["1s1", "2p1"], corrections="none")
   a.solve()
   print(a.result.orbital_energies)   # 2p1/2 ≈ -0.3616 Ha
   # exact Dirac 2p1/2: -0.125002081 Ha
   ```

   The same call with `configs=["1s1"]` returns the exact 1s energy, so the defect appears as soon as a second configuration gives an unoccupied channel a fractional Guerra population.

2. **Breit + QED cannot improve a CI excitation energy** (maximum level-by-level difference between `MCDHF` and `MCDHF+B+QED` over the whole panel: 1.49e-08 cm⁻¹). This is documented in the MCDHF docstring; the panel confirms it independently.
